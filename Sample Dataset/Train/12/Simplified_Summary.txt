When people disagree about what happened, and the court has to decide who is telling the truth, the appeals court should usually trust the original trial judge. The appeals court should only disagree if the trial judge missed something important about a witness or if the story is so unlikely that it can't be true.  The trial judge doesn't have to say they based their decision on how the witnesses acted in court.  However, this is just a general rule. It doesn't mean the first court is always right about who is telling the truth or being honest.  [In a case where the High Court disagreed with the trial court's decision based on spoken evidence, the Supreme Court said the High Court was wrong. The High Court thought the rule about trusting the trial judge didn't apply because the trial judge didn't say they based their decision on how the witnesses seemed. But the Supreme Court looked at all the evidence and changed the High Court's decision.] W.C.
