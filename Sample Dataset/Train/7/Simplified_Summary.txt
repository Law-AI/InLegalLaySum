If someone dies from injuries caused by a deadly weapon, the people bringing the case (the prosecution) must show that the weapon could have caused the injuries in the way they claim. They usually need an expert to prove this.  For example, in a murder case, the prosecution said the accused person shot the victim with a gun. But it looked like the injuries were from a rifle. There was no expert to say the gun could have caused the injuries. Also, the injuries suggested more than one person fired shots, but the prosecution only accused one person. The High Court knew the prosecution's evidence was lacking but still found the accused guilty based on witness statements.  The Supreme Court decided this was wrong. The case was like one they had seen before (Pritam Singh vs The State). The accused person was found guilty even though the prosecution didn't prove a key part of their case. So, the conviction was overturned.  The Supreme Court also said that when someone claims they were somewhere else (an alibi), the proof needed is the same as for the prosecution's case. Both sides need to provide a reasonable level of proof.
