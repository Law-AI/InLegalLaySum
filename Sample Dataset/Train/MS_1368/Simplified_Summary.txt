The Calcutta High Court ordered two major government agencies, the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and the National Investigation Agency (NIA), to investigate a claim. This claim involved forced religious changes in West Bengal's Malda district.  Judge Rajasekhar Mantha was hearing a request from two women. They said their husbands, who were brothers and lived in Kaliachak, were forced to change their religion from Hinduism to Islam. The women believed this happened as punishment because their husbands worked for a political party that lost the last election.  So, the Court asked these agencies for information about several claims. These included forced religious changes, people illegally crossing the border, and threats. There were also claims of frightening behavior caused by storing many weapons and fake money.  The Court ordered the NIA and CBI to provide their information. Since they were already part of this legal case, they needed to respond to the claims made in the official petition.  Also, the top police officer in Malda, the Superintendent of Police (SP), was told to work with the CBI and NIA.  The women, who are sisters, said their husbands disappeared on November 24 of last year. They claimed the police had not done anything about it, even after being told. But the state government argued that the two men willingly changed to Islam and left home due to a family disagreement.  After hearing both sides, the Court noted it was surprising. The police station had not filed official reports (FIRs) or taken any action, which is required by a Supreme Court ruling.  Because of this, the Court told the Malda Police Superintendent to separately give a detailed report. This report should cover the women's claims, what the police had done, or any other important information. This information was needed to decide the problems mentioned in the petition. The SP was also ordered to check the women's safety and take immediate steps, as they feared serious danger to their lives.  The order also said that the SP should create a detailed timeline of events. This timeline should cover what happened before and after the women filed their complaints.  The Court also stated that the agencies should look into other claims made by the women. These included widespread forced conversions, people illegally crossing the border, and threats. They also included frightening behavior, storing many weapons, and fake money.  The Court further explained that these other claims might not be directly about the women's main case. However, they seemed very connected to the claims that their husbands were kidnapped and forced to convert.  Therefore, the case was scheduled for another hearing on June 21.
