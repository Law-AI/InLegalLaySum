Last week, a high court in India, the Allahabad High Court in Lucknow, denied a request from Abbas Ansari. This request was for "anticipatory bail," which is a special type of bail. It means he asked the court to promise he would not be arrested, even before any charges were officially brought against him. Ansari is a state politician, an MLA, and the son of another jailed politician, Mukhtar Ansari. This case is about his gun permits.  The judge, Justice Dinesh Kumar Singh, refused the bail. He noted that Ansari had been avoiding court dates. The court had even issued a public order, called a "proclamation," telling him to appear.  The court considered serious accusations against Ansari. They said he unfairly got his gun permit and bought many illegal gun parts, weapons, and bullets. He claimed these were for shooting sports. However, these weapons and bullets are not allowed in shooting practice and go against a government rule from 2014. Also, because Ansari has been ignoring court orders, the court saw no reason to give him anticipatory bail.  The case in brief Ansari has been charged with several crimes, including cheating, forgery, and breaking gun laws. The accusations state that he moved his gun permit from Lucknow to New Delhi. He did this without telling officials in Lucknow or the local police. The claim is that he intended to buy and use guns unlawfully.  It is also claimed that Ansari used this permit to buy and register many guns illegally. Police found over 4,400 bullets with him, and many were "metal-jacketed." For a sport shooter, having these types of bullets for practice is against the law and against the rules set by the International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF).  There are also claims that Ansari did not use the proper permit from the National Rifle Association of India (NRAI) to bring in a rifle and six gun barrels. Instead, he brought them in his personal luggage. He paid a lower tax meant for personal imports, which was not the correct way to import these items.  Earlier this month, a lower court judge, who handles cases involving politicians, had already turned down Ansari’s request for anticipatory bail. Ansari then appealed to the High Court. He argued that he was being unfairly targeted because he is Mukhtar Ansari's son. He claimed it was for political reasons, as the current government is against his family.  Court's observations First, the High Court noted that a lower judge had sent several official notices, called "summons," to Ansari between January 2021 and January 2022. However, he never showed up in court for his trial.  The court also pointed out that "bailable warrants" were issued in April and May 2022. These are arrest orders where a person can pay money to be released. But Ansari still did not appear. After that, "non-bailable warrants" were issued in July and August 2022, which meant he couldn't just pay to be released. Still, he did not turn himself in. With no other choice, the court issued a public announcement in August 2022, ordering him to appear.  Because of all these issues, the court denied his request for anticipatory bail. However, the court did say that if Ansari turns himself in to the lower court and asks for regular bail (which happens after an arrest), his request should be handled quickly and fairly according to the law.
